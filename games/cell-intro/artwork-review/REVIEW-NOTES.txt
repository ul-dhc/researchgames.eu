CELL ARTWORK — CURRENT ADOPTED PLANT MODEL AND UPCOMING CELLS
Updated 27 October 2026. This standalone gallery is for artwork review.
The playable expansion remains local; the public playable introduction is unchanged.

1. ADOPTED PLANT MODEL — artwork/original-onion-model.png
The established original onion cell model is the current Plant identity.
This transparent preview is rendered directly from the existing SVG cell paths
and gradients, with the original organic-nucleus-warm.png placed at the model’s
original nucleus position. No new onion artwork or anatomy was generated.
The local chooser and completion backdrop use this original model. The Plant
journey retains its original microscope field, reconstructed cell reveal,
SVG cell model, surrounding-cell context and original nucleus throughout.
Earlier onion-epidermal and onion-epidermal-transparent files remain archived
assets only; the v2 onion candidate is not the currently adopted Plant identity.

2. UPCOMING HUMAN CHEEK CELL — artwork/human-cheek-epithelial-transparent.png
Newer approved artwork is unchanged. Muted peach and cream, with a lilac nucleus;
a flattened animal-cell example without a plant wall or large central vacuole.
Internal detail is idealised for learning. The journey remains Upcoming.

3. UPCOMING ESCHERICHIA COLI — artwork/ecoli-transparent.png
Newer approved artwork is unchanged. Sage-grey rod with cytoplasm, ribosomes,
nucleoid DNA and schematic flagella. No nucleus or membrane-bound organelles.
The journey remains Upcoming.

TRANSPARENCY AND PROVENANCE
Plant PNG/WebP files are source-derived renders of the established model.
Human/E. coli transparent PNGs retain their approved original colour pixels;
only background alpha was extracted. Their artwork files were not changed in
this gallery update. Earlier masters and review candidates are preserved.

QUESTIONS FOR SCIENTIFIC REVIEW
Are the structures and their spatial relationships credible and readable?
Is the human cheek-cell representation suitable for this learning purpose?
Does the E. coli envelope, nucleoid or flagellar depiction need correction?
Can a beginner distinguish a nucleus from the bacterial nucleoid?

CONVENTIONS
These are educational cutaways, not microscope photographs. Separate display
magnifications are used; equal card widths do not indicate equal biological
sizes. Colour, translucency and enlarged detail support readability.
