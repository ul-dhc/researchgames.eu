THREE CELLS — SCIENTIFIC AND VISUAL REVIEW
Selected artwork: approved refinement version 2, with transparent-background derivatives, 26 October 2026.
Background alpha has been removed using image-editing mattes; the original approved colour pixels, canvas and anatomical detail are preserved. Original opaque files remain archived.

Purpose
These illustrations will introduce three explorable cells before the Switch Lab
molecular-biology game. They share a softly shaded drawing style, with distinct
but related palettes. These are AI-generated educational cutaways, not recorded
microscope photographs. Equal display sizes represent separate magnifications.
This package is for artwork review, not a completed three-route game.

1. ONION BULB EPIDERMAL CELL — artwork/onion-epidermal-transparent.png
Warm ivory, taupe and lilac. The intended structures are a cell wall outside the
membrane, a large central vacuole, thin peripheral cytoplasm and a peripheral
nucleus. Onion bulb epidermis should not be filled with chloroplasts.
The proposed direction is to use this same cell illustration consistently in
both the cell-selection screen and the existing onion exploration.

2. LIVING HUMAN CHEEK EPITHELIAL CELL — artwork/human-cheek-epithelial-transparent.png
Muted peach and cream, with a lilac nucleus. Intended as a flattened, irregular
animal-cell example, without a plant cell wall or large central plant vacuole.
The nucleus and surrounding organelles are deliberately legible. Please assess
whether this is a defensible representative living cheek epithelial cell; its
internal detail is idealised rather than a claim about one observed specimen.

3. ESCHERICHIA COLI — artwork/ecoli-transparent.png
Mineral sage and grey. Rod-shaped envelope, cytoplasm, ribosomes and nucleoid DNA.
No nucleus, nuclear membrane, mitochondria or other membrane-bound organelles.
The three flagella depict a schematic motile representative; their exact number
and placement are not intended as a universal characteristic of E. coli strains.

QUESTIONS FOR REVIEW
- Are the main structures and their spatial relationships credible?
- Are any structures misleading, missing or unnecessarily prominent?
- Is the human cheek-cell choice suitable for this learning purpose?
- Does the E. coli envelope/nucleoid/flagellar depiction need correction?
- Can a beginner distinguish the three silhouettes and identify the nucleus
  where present, without mistaking the bacterial nucleoid for a nucleus?
- Which corrections are necessary before these illustrations enter the game?

Illustration conventions
Colour, translucency and cutaways serve identification and readability. Fine
features are enlarged. Shared structures should retain recognisable identities
as players move between cells. Anatomical review is requested before final use.
